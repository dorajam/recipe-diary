Product Requirements · v0.3 Personal Project · 2026

From saved links to a cookbook.

A workshop for recipes I collect, cook, and refine — built to move a pile of Instagram saves toward a book I've actually made, one week at a time.

The Problem

I save constantly. I retrieve never.

I save a lot of recipes on Instagram, but they're not searchable — so when it's time to cook, I can't find anything, and I never end up making them.

The saving is easy and already happening. The failure is at retrieval: the moment I want a "quick weeknight Korean dinner" or "something with the eggplant in the fridge," my saved recipes are a scroll-only graveyard. The archive exists; it just can't be asked questions.

But there's a deeper failure underneath. Even when I can find a recipe, nothing moves me to make it — and the real goal is to cook enough of my own, tested recipes to eventually write a cookbook. So this can't only be an archive. It has to get me into action-mode: cook something every week, and keep the ones worth keeping.

The Core Idea

A pipeline, not a shoebox.

Every recipe has a status, and the whole app exists to move recipes rightward — from a link I dumped, to a dish I've made and would put in the book. The archive isn't the destination; it's the mouth of the funnel.

Stage 01
Saved
A link or manual entry lands in the collection. Low effort, high volume.
Stage 02
Planned
I commit to it — added to a specific week's short "cook next" list.
Stage 03
Cooked
I made it, and logged how it went + what I changed, while fresh.
Stage 04
Book candidate
Cooked, loved, refined — flagged as good enough for the cookbook.

Collect fills stage 01 Cook drives 02 → 03 → 04 The book is everything at stage 04

The Two Halves

Collect and Cook, co-equal.

These are the two engines, of equal weight. Collect is useless without Cook (a searchable pile I never make), and Cook starves without Collect (nothing to plan). One feeds; the other produces.

Half A · Collect

Capture & retrieve

Fills the funnel and makes it answerable.

  • Save by link (Instagram / website) or manual entry — fast, tag-later.
  • Name + one thumbnail + my custom tags.
  • Multi-dimensional search & full-text, so any question gets an answer.
Half B · Cook

Plan → Make → Log

The engine that produces the cookbook.

  • Each week, pull 1–3 recipes into "This Week" — a small, real commitment.
  • Mark cooked; log rating, tweaks, and a photo while it's fresh.
  • Promote the keepers to book candidates and watch the list grow.
MVP Scope · Must-Haves

The smallest thing that runs the whole loop.

  1. Save a recipe
    Add by sharing a link (Instagram or website) or by manual entry. Name + a single thumbnail. Custom tags, all optional — a recipe is useful with just a name.
  2. Search across many dimensions
    Filter and full-text search across the tag system below, in any combination.
    Meal breakfast · lunch · dinner · snack · dessert Ingredient Technique Flavor sour · spicy · … Keyword fresh · cozy · high-protein · … Cuisine moroccan · japanese · korean · … Source who gave it to me Full-text
  3. Plan the week ★ THE ACTION DRIVER
    Pick 1–3 recipes into a "This Week" list — the deliberate act that turns browsing into cooking. A shortlist, not a calendar. This is the feature that produces the cookbook, so it's a must-have, not a nice-to-have.
  4. Cooked it — with notes
    Mark a recipe as made, and log the result: rating, what I modified, a photo. My memory across attempts, and the raw material for book candidates.
  5. Track status toward the book
    Each recipe shows where it is — saved, planned, cooked, or book candidate — so I can see progress accumulating and flag the keepers.
Designing Against Friction

The two ways this dies — and the antidotes.

Both of my worries are really the same disease: the loop stalls before it builds momentum. Two failure points, each with a designed-in antidote.

Risk 1 · The cold start — too few recipes to bother searching

If the app starts empty, there's nothing to find, so I stop opening it, so it stays empty. The spiral kills it in week one.

  • Seed it in one sitting. A one-time bulk import of my existing Instagram saves / bookmarks, so day one starts with ~40 recipes, not zero. An empty app is a chore; a full one is a toy.
  • Saving is a 5-second reflex. Paste link → save. Tagging is optional and deferrable — never a form standing between me and saving.
  • A reason to open beyond saving. The weekly "plan this week" ritual pulls me in even on weeks I saved nothing.

Risk 2 · The inertia — I have recipes but never cook them

Searching is passive; cooking is the hard, active step. Without a nudge, the collection stays admired and unused — and the cookbook never gets written.

  • Planning is the forcing function. A weekly prompt to commit 1–3 recipes to "This Week" converts intention into a plan. This is why planning is a co-equal must-have, not a side feature.
  • Log while it's fresh. Cooking → immediate, easy logging makes progress visible and turns each cook into a step toward a book candidate.
  • Visible progress. A growing count of "book candidates" is the scoreboard that keeps the habit rewarding.
Boundaries

What this is — and pointedly isn't.

In Scope · MVP
  • Single-user. Just me. I add friends' recipes manually — no accounts for them.
  • Save via link or manual entry, with name, thumbnail, and optional custom tags.
  • Multi-dimensional filtering + full-text search.
  • "This Week" planning shortlist — the weekly cook commitment.
  • "Cooked it" log with per-attempt notes, modifications, and a photo.
  • Status pipeline: saved → planned → cooked → book candidate.
Out of Scope
  • Collaboration — commenting or co-editing recipes. Deliberately cut. A friend's recipe is just an entry I type in.
  • A social network for discovering other people.
  • A full meal planner. "This Week" is a 1–3 recipe shortlist, not a 7-day calendar with portions and schedules.
  • Grocery-list planning.
Success Metrics

How I'll know it's working.

North Star

I cook one recipe a week from my own collection.

The behavior everything is built to drive — and the raw output the book is made of.

Outcome

The book-candidate count grows every month.

The funnel is producing. This is the metric that proves the app serves the real goal.

Leading

I plan a week ~1× a week, and save into it instead of Instagram.

The habit inputs that make the north star happen.

PM flag · biggest remaining risk

Manual tagging is where Collect quietly fails.

Search only works if recipes are well-tagged, and I've chosen to tag by hand across seven dimensions. The likely failure mode: I skip tagging while saving in a hurry, recipes become un-findable, and I'm back to the Instagram graveyard. Mitigation baked into scope: tags are optional, deferrable, and fast — quick-add chips, reuse/autocomplete — and a recipe stays useful with just a name. Worth revisiting whether a touch of save-time assist would de-risk this further.
